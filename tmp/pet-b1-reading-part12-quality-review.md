### Test 14 Part 2

**Profile 1:** Elliot works late and wants an indoor practical beginner course, with a medium budget and something to take home. They also want the practical arrangements to be predictable and do not want to purchase specialist equipment.

**Profile 2:** Farah is free Saturday mornings, owns gloves, likes outdoor group work and needs a cheap activity near the centre. A nearby meeting point matters because they do not want a long journey after their other commitments.

**Profile 3:** Grace has one free lunchtime hour and wants free indoor conversation practice without homework or formal teaching. They prefer an informal option where missing one session will not prevent them from continuing later.

**Profile 4:** Hugo uses a wheelchair, owns a phone and wants a small-group photography activity with a step-free route. Comfort and straightforward access are more important than a competitive or highly demanding programme.

**Profile 5:** Isla wants a recognised qualification before volunteering at festivals, can pay and is happy with practical pair work. The final result should be useful for a future plan, rather than simply being a one-off social activity.

**Options**

- A. An evening beginner pottery course at the arts centre. Clay and tools are supplied, and finished work can be collected later. The moderate fee is paid per course; sessions end at nine. Sessions include a clear introduction, a practical task and time to ask questions before participants leave. The timetable is published clearly so learners can plan their visit.
- B. A Saturday morning gardening club beside the community centre. Members work outdoors in shared plots, bring gloves and learn from experienced growers. It is inexpensive and social. The organiser confirms places in advance and explains the meeting point, expected costs and what members should bring. Clear instructions are sent before the first meeting.
- C. A free weekday lunchtime language exchange in a quiet library room. Adults practise conversation with changing partners, without formal teaching or homework. The activity is designed for people who value a manageable commitment, clear information and a friendly atmosphere. It is suitable for learners who want steady progress.
- D. A monthly family craft afternoon at the museum. Materials are included and activities are indoors, but children need an accompanying adult. Participants receive simple guidance at the start, then choose an activity that matches their own experience and aims. The organiser explains the timetable before work begins.
- E. An accessible photography walk for adults. The leader chooses a short step-free route and pauses often; participants need a phone or camera. The leader checks everyone’s needs beforehand and adapts the pace so the group can finish comfortably and safely. Questions are welcomed throughout the activity.
- F. A free Tuesday repair workshop. Skilled members help mend small household items, but visitors bring the broken item and cannot book appointments. Visitors should contact the organiser first, because the service has limited space and follows a set weekly timetable. This prevents an unnecessary journey when no place remains.
- G. A quiet monthly book group at a village hall. Members read one agreed novel, but the hall is outside town and transport is self-arranged. Members receive the reading or learning material before meeting and are encouraged to contribute at their own pace. New members can ask for a short explanation.
- H. A weekend first-aid course with a certificate and practical pair work. It costs more than clubs but suits future event volunteers. The tutor explains the practical requirements clearly and gives participants a record of what they have completed. The certificate is useful when arranging future volunteering work.

**Profile 4:** Victor is an experienced hiker with camping equipment, wants a remote challenge and can catch an early train. Comfort and straightforward access are more important than a competitive or highly demanding programme.

**Profile 5:** Wendy wants a weekend wildlife photography trip with vegetarian food and shared transport; she owns a camera and has no young children. The final result should be useful for a future plan, rather than simply being a one-off social activity.

**Options**

- A. A half-day beginner kayak trip on calm water. Boats and safety equipment are supplied, the launch is beside the bus station, and groups are small; participants must swim confidently. Sessions include a clear introduction, a practical task and time to ask questions before participants leave. The timetable is published clearly so learners can plan their visit.
- B. A demanding two-day mountain walk for experienced hikers. Walkers carry food, tents and equipment; the remote start is reached by a morning train. The organiser confirms places in advance and explains the meeting point, expected costs and what members should bring. Clear instructions are sent before the first meeting.
- C. A low-cost coastal cycle route with bike hire. Riders use quiet lanes between villages and return before evening; the route is mostly flat but lunch is self-arranged. The activity is designed for people who value a manageable commitment, clear information and a friendly atmosphere. It is suitable for learners who want steady progress.
- D. A wildlife photography weekend at a forest lodge. The guide teaches observation, vegetarian meals are provided and guests share a minibus; it is not for very young children. Participants receive simple guidance at the start, then choose an activity that matches their own experience and aims. The organiser explains the timetable before work begins.
- E. A responsible city walk led by residents. It supports independent businesses, is accessible and affordable, stays on foot and has indoor stops for bad weather. The leader checks everyone’s needs beforehand and adapts the pace so the group can finish comfortably and safely. Questions are welcomed throughout the activity.
- F. A family beach-clean morning. Gloves and bags are supplied, children join with an adult and the work ends with a talk about reducing plastic waste. Visitors should contact the organiser first, because the service has limited space and follows a set weekly timetable. This prevents an unnecessary journey when no place remains.
- G. A luxury island boat excursion with lunch and transfers. It costs a lot and follows a fixed timetable, with little independent exploring. Members receive the reading or learning material before meeting and are encouraged to contribute at their own pace. New members can ask for a short explanation.
- H. A free self-guided moorland route with audio stories. It is flexible, but has no transport or equipment hire and limited signal high up. The tutor explains the practical requirements clearly and gives participants a record of what they have completed. The certificate is useful when arranging future volunteering work.
