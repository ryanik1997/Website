### Part 4 – Starting a tree-planting project

When the farmer offered a strip of unused land beside the village, a group of residents saw a chance to create a small woodland. They had discussed the idea for years, but never had a suitable site. (16) ..... The first step was to check whether the soil could support young trees. The offer transformed a vague ambition into a practical plan. The farmer said he was happy the land would serve a long-term purpose.

A local ecologist visited the site and confirmed that native species such as oak and birch would grow well. She also warned that the first two years would require regular watering and protection from rabbits. (17) ..... The volunteers agreed to share these tasks on a rota basis. This advice meant the volunteers had to commit beyond a single weekend. Several volunteers bought watering cans and stored them in their sheds.

Funding came from a council grant and a sponsored walk organised by the primary school. The money covered saplings, tree guards and basic tools. (18) ..... Without this support, the project would have remained a discussion rather than becoming a reality. The grant arrived quickly because the school’s walk had raised public interest. A local newspaper printed a photograph of the schoolchildren on the walk.

On the first planting day, thirty people turned up despite cold wind. Children carried saplings while adults dug holes, and the ecologist checked each tree before soil was replaced. (19) ..... By lunchtime, forty trees stood in neat rows along the boundary. The shared effort made the work feel achievable despite the weather. Everyone agreed that the cold made the hot soup afterwards taste better.

Two years later, most of the trees had survived, and the first birds were nesting in the taller saplings. The volunteers still meet monthly to clear weeds and check guards. (20) ..... The strip of land that once grew only nettles now has a name: the Millennium Wood. The project had become part of the village’s identity. Residents who had never volunteered before now ask when the next session is.
